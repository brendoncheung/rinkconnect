# First conversation with the coordinator

One page. The full list is in
[questions-for-the-club.md](questions-for-the-club.md) — that's a reference,
not a script. This is what the first conversation should actually be.

---

## The one rule

**Do not show her the app.**

She already loved the pitch. If she sees a design, you get enthusiasm, and
enthusiasm has been the least informative signal in this entire project.
Everything you need is in what she already does. Show her something at the
*second* conversation, once you know what she does.

---

## Open with this

> **"Walk me through the last event you ran, start to finish."**

Then stay quiet and let her talk. Follow where it goes rather than steering:

- How did people find out about it?
- Where did the yeses arrive — text, email, in person at the rink?
- How did you keep track of who was coming?
- How did you know afterwards who actually showed up, and for how long?
- What was the most annoying part?

## Then ask to see things

Worth more than anything she says about them:

1. **The member list.** The one she'd type into RinkConnect. Look at the
   columns. *Does it have one email per family, or one per parent?* That single
   detail decides the account model.
2. **Wherever she tracks hours now.** Spreadsheet, notebook, phone notes.
   This is your actual competition — research found other clubs run this on a
   Google Form and a spreadsheet, which is free and already works.
3. **The last event's volunteer signup**, in whatever form it exists.
4. **The June 23 minutes**, if she has them. The July agenda references them,
   and how volunteer hours get discussed at board level is currently
   documented nowhere but in conversation.

---

## Three questions that must get answered

Everything else can wait. These are load-bearing:

**1. If two separated parents each have their own membership for the same
skater — is that one hours obligation, or two?**

*The only question that could invalidate a decision already made. Everything
is built around one balance per season membership.*

**2. What happens when someone signs up for a shift and then doesn't show, or
backs out?**

*An abandoned slot is worse for her than one nobody claimed, because she
stopped looking for cover. Cheap to design for now, painful later.*

**3. What would have to be true for you to stop keeping this in your head?**

*Either answer teaches you something. If she can name it, that's your spec. If
she can't, the memory isn't actually the pain and you should find out what is.*

---

## Also confirm, in passing

- Is she in fact the volunteer coordinator? (The agenda lists her against the
  Volunteer Report; nothing has confirmed it.)
- Is she comfortable being the one who *sets* each family's hours, or does the
  board set a figure she applies?
- How does the fee for unfinished hours actually get charged — added to next
  season's registration, invoiced separately, something else?

---

## Bring back

- The member list's actual columns
- Whatever she uses today, in whatever state it's in
- Her answer to question 1, verbatim if possible

Those three are enough to either confirm the eight ADRs or break one, and
breaking one now is much cheaper than breaking it after a season of real
balances.
